# PIWCO – Please Invest With Confidence

**A mobile game that teaches investing and credit by letting you make real money decisions, see the results in your own city and try again smarter.**

Collabothon 2026 · Commerzbank challenge **"Finance Quest"** · theme **"Innovations for all generations"**

## ▶️ Play it now

**In the browser: https://apaczus.github.io/piwco/** (nothing to install)

- **On a computer:** the app runs inside a phone frame. Click and drag like on a phone (swipe the lesson cards, drag the sliders). Sound is on; the speaker icon turns it off.
- **On a phone:** open the same link or scan the QR code on the page. It opens full screen in the phone's browser. Direct link: https://apaczus.github.io/piwco/app/
- **Fastest path to the main mission:** in the survey rate your finance knowledge **3–5**. You go straight to the investing chapter (August). Or tap **"Skip to game ›"** (top right of the survey) to start with a demo player (a small restaurant owner who knows the basics).
- **Two kinds of players:** choose **entrepreneur** (pick a business: Gastronomy, IT & Software or Manufacturing) or **private person** (investing and credit, no company).
- Progress lives in the app's memory: refreshing the page starts a new game. Haptics work only in the native app.

## The challenge and our answer

Commerzbank asked how to make financial learning **engaging, relevant and actionable**, so that self-directed investors don't just read about finance but feel confident enough to **take the first step**.

Our answer: **try first, then learn, then try again.** In PIWCO you don't read a course. You make a real decision with your own (game) money. You see what happens and learn why in a short swipe lesson. Then you get a second chance to do it better, and your city grows with every good decision.

## Target learner

- **Primary:** small business owners and self-employed people who earn money but don't invest it. They mix company and private money, choose loans by the lowest installment and leave savings on an account where inflation eats them.
- **Also:** private persons of any age who know the basics but haven't made their first investment yet.
- **Different starting points:** a short survey adapts the game to the player: age, income, savings, home, car, business industry and a **finance knowledge self-rating 1–5**.

## Learning objectives

After the main mission (the investing chapter) the player can:

1. Tell the difference between a **stock, a bond, an ETF, a deposit and cash**, and their return and risk.
2. Explain **why diversification matters**: one company vs an S&P 500 ETF.
3. See with their own numbers that **doing nothing costs money**: 10 000 PLN on an account loses about 39% of its buying power in 10 years at 5% inflation.
4. Understand that **time in the market and small regular top-ups** (100 PLN a month) beat trying to time the market.

The credit lessons add: the real cost of debt and **APR (RRSO)**, **good vs bad debt**, **creditworthiness (BIK)**, comparing two loan offers, **stress-testing** debt, **reference rates (WIBOR)**, **consolidation** and **liquidity**.

## The complete mission: "Grow 10 000 PLN without fear" (investing chapter)

| Step | What the player does | What they learn |
|---|---|---|
| **1. First try** | Splits 10 000 PLN between 5 instruments with sliders: account, savings deposit, treasury bonds, S&P 500 ETF, one risky company (NovaTech). Each card shows return and risk, and "i" explains what can go wrong. | Making a real decision before any theory. |
| **2. One year, fast** | Watches the year play out in a few seconds on a chart, with market news popping up: "NovaTech −45%: product recall", "Markets fell 12%", "Prices rose 5%". | Volatility and risk feel real. |
| **3. Result** | Sees the result, its value after inflation, every alternative on one chart and "what could have happened". | The first try usually goes badly, and the chart shows how much better it could have been. |
| **4. Swipe lesson** | 12 short cards: stock, bond, ETF, diversification, a choice card (NovaTech vs S&P 500 ETF), the big indices (+ "Read more": liquidity, volume, P/E…), short vs long term, "money melts" (inflation), start small, and a myth/fact quiz. | The concepts, explained with the player's own numbers. |
| **5. Second try** | Chooses a horizon (1 / 5 / 10 years), splits the money again and adds a monthly top-up. | Applying what they just learned. |
| **6. Compare** | Sees Try 1 vs Try 2 side by side: "+X PLN better than your first try". | Confidence: "I can do this." |
| **7. Reward** | The **Future tower** in their city grows, one floor per finished step. | Visible progress. |

The results are honest: the market is a fixed, deterministic 10-year path, so **Try 2 is better only if the player's decisions are better**, not because of luck. Money paid in through top-ups doesn't count as a win: the comparison uses the gain after what was paid in, in today's money.

## How the experience supports understanding and engagement

- **Decide before you learn.** The lesson explains a result the player just felt, not abstract theory.
- **Short, swipeable content.** Cards are one idea each: info cards, myth vs fact, good vs bad debt sorting, choice and quiz cards, about 2 minutes per lesson.
- **One screen, one decision.** Big cards, one main button, clear titles that say what to do.
- **Your own city as a progress map.** Home, Company, Cushion vault, Protection and the Future tower grow with good decisions, crack under debt and get rain clouds with unpaid bills.
- **A second chance.** Try again until you beat your first result; any finished month can be reviewed or replayed. Mistakes are part of learning, not a game over.
- **Feedback that feels good:** rolling numbers, charts that draw themselves, level-up animations, confetti, sounds and haptics (respecting the "reduce motion" setting).

## Fit with "Innovations for all generations"

- **Different levels of experience:** players who rate their knowledge 1–2 start with the basics (January: budget and cash flow). Players who rate 3–5 skip them and go straight to investing and credit.
- **Different life stages:** age bands from 18–25 to 65+, entrepreneurs and private persons, owning or renting a home, with or without a car and savings.
- **Different financial circumstances:** the starting money scales with the player's income and savings from the survey.
- **Different investment horizons:** the second try lets the player choose 1, 5 or 10 years. The "Start small" card shows that age and budget are not an excuse.

## Technology

The app is built in **TypeScript** with **React Native + Expo** (Expo Router, react-native-svg, expo-haptics, expo-audio, Manrope font), running on Android, iOS (Expo Go) and the web (react-native-web). The game logic is a separate **deterministic TypeScript engine** (seeded random generator, month-end settlement, loans with interest, portfolio simulation with inflation) covered by **Vitest** tests. All content (lessons, events, numbers) lives in **JSON** files that non-programmers can edit. The whole game runs on the device, so the web version is a static site: GitHub Actions builds it and publishes it on GitHub Pages. A **Fastify + PostgreSQL** backend is prepared for later (leaderboard, server-side result check).

| Folder | What |
|---|---|
| `apps/mobile` | Expo / React Native app (iOS, Android, web): onboarding, January, investing chapter, credit lessons, city |
| `packages/engine` | Game engine: pure TypeScript, deterministic simulation, with tests |
| `packages/content` | Game content as JSON: curriculum (12 months), lessons, events, instruments, loan products, onboarding |
| `apps/server` | Fastify backend (only `GET /health` so far) |
| `docker-compose.yml` | PostgreSQL 18 (not used by the code yet) |
| `docs/invest-texts.md` | Every investing-chapter text and number in one place, for review |

Built during the hackathon by our team, with **Claude Code** as an AI coding assistant. Project decisions and progress are documented in [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md) (read it first if you want to continue the project).

## Run it yourself

Requirements: **Node.js 24** and npm 11; **Expo Go** on the phone for the native app (latest version from the App Store / Google Play).

```bash
git clone https://github.com/apaczus/piwco.git
cd piwco
npm install
```

Check that everything works:

```bash
npm test -w @piwco/engine
npm test -w @piwco/mobile
npm run typecheck -w @piwco/mobile
```

Run the app (phone and computer on the same Wi-Fi), then scan the QR code: with the camera on iPhone, from inside Expo Go on Android:

```bash
npm run start -w @piwco/mobile
```

Other options:

- Phones on another network: `npm run start -w @piwco/mobile -- --tunnel` (Expo may ask to install its tunnel package). With Tailscale on the computer and the phones (Linux/macOS): `npm run start:tailscale -w @piwco/mobile -- --clear`.
- Changes in the code reach the phones by themselves (dev mode). Production mode (`-- --no-dev --minify`) is smoother, but Expo Go keeps a cached old version after changes. Use it only for a final demo.
- Web version locally (same as GitHub Actions): `npm run build:web -w @piwco/mobile` → `apps/mobile/dist` (the page with the phone frame at the root, the app in `app/`, base URL `/piwco/app`). It is built and published automatically from `master` by `.github/workflows/web.yml`.
- "Skip to game ›" in the survey (asks first, then starts with demo data) can be turned off in `apps/mobile/src/devFlags.ts`.
- Backend: `npm run dev -w @piwco/server` (http://0.0.0.0:3000/health). Postgres: `docker compose up -d --wait` (127.0.0.1:5433, user / password / database: `piwco`; needs Docker).
